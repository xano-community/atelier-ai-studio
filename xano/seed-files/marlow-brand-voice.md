# How Marlow sounds

Marlow writes the way a good mechanic talks: plain, warm and a little dry. We say what the bike does on a real ride, then give the one number that matters, exactly once.

## The rules

- Short sentences. One idea each.
- Ride first, spec second. "It climbs without fuss" before "66.5° head angle".
- Give a spec once, exactly, with its unit: 2.4 in tyres, 13.9 kg, 8-speed hub.
- Talk to one rider, as "you". We are "we", never "Marlow believes".
- Be honest about who a bike isn't for. It earns trust for everything else.
- British spelling: colour, tyre, aluminium.

## Words we never use

game-changer, unleash, epic, best-in-class, revolutionary, next-level, crush, beast.

If a line needs one of these to work, the line is wrong. Say what actually happens instead.

## Good and bad lines

| Instead of | We write |
|---|---|
| Unleash your inner adventurer with the epic Trail 2. | The Trail 2 likes rough ground. So will you. |
| A best-in-class commuting experience. | Upright, quiet and dry-chained. The City 3 gets you to work in a good mood. |
| Game-changing tyre clearance! | Room for 2.4 in tyres, with mud to spare. |

## Writing about sizing

Riders worry about sizing more than anything else. Answer the worry directly, then point to the size guide.

- The Trail 2 rides long. Between a medium and a large, take the medium.
- Never say a bike "fits everyone". Give the height range for each frame.

## Writing about the warranty

Every Marlow frame has a lifetime warranty for the first owner. Say it plainly and early: "The frame is covered for as long as you own it." Don't hedge it with "terms apply" in the headline; the terms belong on the warranty page.

## Sound and pictures

Voiceovers are calm, friendly and at a medium pace. Music is warm indie, around 100 bpm, no vocals. Photographs use natural light in real places. Bikes are ridden or leaning, never floating, and never with lens flare.
